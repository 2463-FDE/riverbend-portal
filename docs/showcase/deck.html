Week 1The AI feature

The assistant works. What matters is what it refuses to do.

Accepted

Withheld

The same summary, plus: “continue taking your metformin 500 mg as prescribed.”

The score barely moved. A summary that is 95% right and invents one medication is more dangerous than an obviously wrong one, because it reads as competent.

So we don’t rely on the score. The system looks for clinical claims that aren’t in the source text, and any hit withholds the answer outright. Your previous vendor’s own transcript contained exactly this failure.

Week 4The finding

Any login could read any patient’s chart.

Your handover included a browser recording. In it, one logged-in patient requests chart 1042, gets it — then requests chart 1043, a different person, and gets that too.

Chart numbers are sequential. Anyone with a login could have walked the entire patient list by changing a number in the address bar.

It was not a forgotten check. Your system had no way to know which patient a login belonged to. “Show me my own record” was not a question the database could answer.
Week 4The fix, and how it’s built

The check runs first. The AI runs last.

0 Records loaded on a denied request — never read, not hidden afterwards
404 Identical to a chart that doesn’t exist, so nobody can probe for real ones
3 of 3 Of her own charts Maria now sees, including the one with her allergy

Permission is decided before anything is fetched, by ordinary code with no AI involved. Each part of the record is then gathered in parallel, each one able to see only what was approved. The AI writes the summary at the end, over material that has already cleared the gate.

The AI is never asked who should see what. That question is answered before it is ever consulted.

What we need from you

Three things, and only one is engineering.

1 · Rotate four credentials

The database password, clearinghouse key, session secret and AI key. They are in the repository’s history and on every copy anyone has ever made. Removing them from the current version does not undo that — only changing them does.

2 · Sign the AWS agreement

A Business Associate Agreement with Amazon, so patient data may legally be processed by their AI service. Everything is built and tested; this is a signature, not a build.

3 · A conversation with the front desk

Coverage now arrives shortly after registration instead of holding it up. Staff need to know that “checking” is not “a problem”, and that a last-known status is usable but provisional. That is a workflow change, and it is theirs to shape.

One question we cannot answer for you: whether anyone ever used the chart access we found. Your audit table can be edited and deleted, so it cannot tell us. That is its own finding, and it is why we would treat this as a risk assessment rather than assume the answer.
